# Checkpoints: what they are, when they happen, and what is done at each one

Prepared for charlie by Capstan, 2026-10-08, against the 26-slide export of 18:08. The word appears on three slides
("The procedure", "What we measure", and "Two generations of cap"), and it means two different things there. This
note explains the main sense first, from the ground up, then separates the other senses so they stop colliding.
Everything is read from the code that ran the streams (`src/pccap/harness/runs.py`), the frozen manifest
(`harness/freeze.py`, `schema.py`), and real output files from the runs.

## 1. The three senses of "checkpoint" in the deck

| Where in the deck | What "checkpoint" means there | Covered in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "The procedure": *At the scheduled checkpoints, test the accumulated memory and preservation*; "What we measure": *scores … at checkpoints 100, 300 and 1,000* | A **pre-declared pause in an editing stream**, after a fixed number of edits, where the cap's whole state is saved to disk and a full read-only re-test is run. This is the sense you asked about. | sections 2 to 8 |
| "Two generations of cap": *the selected checkpoint is called v5* | A **saved copy of the reader's weights during its training run**. The reader was trained for 300 steps and its weights were saved every 50 steps; v5 is a particular combination of those saves. | section 9 |
| "More settling bought retention at a cost": *Legacy 50M ePC base* | A **saved weight file for a base model**, the ePC-trained GPT-2 whose weights were regenerated by the 50-million-token run and hashed into the freeze. | section 9 |

In all three senses the common idea is: *a complete, saved, hashed snapshot of some learnable state at a named moment,
so that the moment can be examined again later without re-running what produced it.* What differs is whose state is
saved and what is done with it.

## 2. From first principles: why a stream needs checkpoints at all

A run (the deck's "procedure") is a stream. Facts are taught one at a time in a fixed order, and after each one the
cap is immediately asked that fact's own prompt. That immediate test answers one question only: *did the cap just
learn this?* It says nothing about whether fact 1 is still intact after facts 2 to 300 have been taught on top of it.

That second question is the whole point of a continual-learning experiment. Teaching new facts can damage old ones
(interference, forgetting) and can damage the base's ordinary behaviour (harm, locality failures). The only way to
measure that is to stop at some point in the stream and re-ask *everything taught so far*, plus a fixed set of prompts
that were never taught, with the cap exactly as it is at that moment.

Doing that after every edit would be prohibitively expensive: re-asking everything after edit *k* costs roughly *k*
prompt evaluations plus their paraphrases, so the total over a 1,000-edit stream would be of order 500,000 extra
generations per cell. Doing it only at the end would hide the trajectory. The compromise is a short list of fixed stop
points, announced before any confirmatory run: these are the checkpoints.

## 3. The schedule and why it was fixed in advance

The checkpoint list is a constant in the runner, `CHECKPOINTS = (100, 300, 1000, 3000)`, and it is also written into
the frozen manifest, where the schema requires it to equal exactly `[100, 300, 1000, 3000]`. Every confirmatory cell
reads the list from the manifest, so no run could use a different schedule.

Only the checkpoints that are at or before the end of the stream fire:

| Stream | Length | Checkpoints that fire | Note |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| zsRE | 1,000 edits | 100, 300, 1,000 | 3,000 was declared in case a longer scope was affordable; it never was |
| CounterFact | 1,000 edits | 100, 300, 1,000 | same |
| MQuAKE | 300 edits | 100, 300 | 300 is both a checkpoint and the end; the deck calls MQuAKE "descriptive" because its 1,000-edit slot is empty |

Fixing the list before the runs is a registration decision, not a convenience. If the stop points were chosen after
seeing results, one could pick the moment where a condition happened to look best. With a registered list, every
condition is examined at the same moments and the primary endpoint (paraphrase retention at the end of the stream) is
read from a moment nobody chose afterwards.

The runner also always records one more pause tagged `end`, whatever the stream length. When the stream length equals
a checkpoint, the two records describe the same state: in the example cell below the `ckpt300` and `end` records carry
the same checkpoint file hash.

## 4. What happens at a checkpoint, step by step

When the number of completed edits reaches a number on the list, the stream pauses and the runner's `rescore`
routine runs. It is read-only: the cap's state hash is recorded and nothing in the cap is allowed to change. In order:

1. **Re-test every fact taught so far on its original prompt.** Each prompt is answered by greedy generation with the
   cap as it is now. A fact counts as retained only if the complete generated answer matches the taught answer
   exactly. The fraction retained is **RET-ES** (own-prompt retention). At checkpoint 300 this re-asks 300 prompts; at
   checkpoint 1,000 it re-asks 1,000.
2. **Re-test every fact on its held-out paraphrases.** Same generation, same exact-match rule, but with rewordings the
   cap never saw during teaching. The mean over paraphrases is **RET-GS** (paraphrase retention), the deck's primary
   endpoint.
3. **Survival conditional on immediate acquisition.** RET-ES restricted to the facts whose immediate test succeeded
   when they were taught. This separates "never learned" from "learned, then lost".
4. **Locality on fixed untaught prompts (LS).** A fixed set of unrelated prompts (200 in the main study), never taught,
   is answered with the cap on and compared with the cap-off base: complete-answer agreement, first-token agreement,
   and the KL divergence between the two next-token distributions at a fixed prefix. This is the "preservation" on the
   slide: does the cap leave alone what it was not asked to change?
5. **Language-model drift on a fixed ordinary-text sample.** A fixed sample of a few thousand ordinary-text positions
   is scored token by token with the cap on and off; the mean loss difference and perplexity ratio are recorded. This
   is a small, in-stream version of the harm measurement; the full 245,237-position harm assay is a separate pass
   (section 6).
6. **Memory accounting.** Bytes allocated and occupied by the record store, per site for the v0 banks.
7. **Save the complete state to disk.** The cap's entire learnable state (every record, every write vector, every key,
   the reader's weights where applicable) is serialised to a file named by the checkpoint, for example
   `learner_ckpt300.ckpt`, under `assets/runs/<cell>/`, and its SHA-256 is recorded. A separate state hash of the
   in-memory object is recorded too, and asserted unchanged after the evaluation.
8. **Charge the cost separately.** The accelerator seconds spent on this pause are recorded as "rescoring" so they
   never mix with the learning cost of the stream itself.

All of that is written into one record per checkpoint in the cell's `checkpoints.json`, alongside the per-fact rows,
so that every headline number can be traced to the fact-level outcomes behind it.

## 5. A timeline of one zsRE cell

```text
edit:   1   2   3  …  100 ──────── 101 … 300 ──────── 301 … 1000 ──────── (end)
        │   │   │      │                    │                     │
   immediate tests     │                    │                     │
   after each edit     ▼                    ▼                     ▼
                  CHECKPOINT 100       CHECKPOINT 300        CHECKPOINT 1,000 (= end)
                  re-ask facts 1–100   re-ask facts 1–300    re-ask facts 1–1,000
                  + paraphrases        + paraphrases         + paraphrases
                  + 200 locality       + 200 locality        + 200 locality
                  + drift sample       + drift sample        + drift sample
                  save learner_ckpt100 save learner_ckpt300  save learner_ckpt1000 / learner_end
```

Teaching resumes after each pause from exactly the state that was saved; the pause changes nothing. For MQuAKE the
diagram stops at 300.

A real record, from one stable-v0 zsRE cell of the confirmatory layout (a 300-edit initial-scope cell), shows the
shape of what is kept:

| Tag | Edits so far | Own-prompt retention | Paraphrase retention | Locality (complete answer) | Rescoring seconds |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| ckpt100 | 100 | 1.000 | 0.460 | 0.995 | 28.6 |
| ckpt300 | 300 | 0.997 | 0.353 | 0.985 | 26.6 |
| end | 300 | 0.997 | 0.353 | 0.985 | 5.5 |

Two things to read off it. First, the checkpoints give a trajectory, not just an endpoint: paraphrase retention of the
first 100 facts was 0.46 when they were the whole memory and the pooled figure fell to 0.35 after 200 more facts were
added. Second, `end` at 300 is the same state as `ckpt300` (identical file hash), so the second rescoring is cheap
because nothing new had to be generated.

## 6. What the saved files are used for afterwards

The saved state is what makes everything after the stream possible without re-running it:

- **The full harm assay.** The 245,237-position ordinary-text measurement on the slide "What we measure" is too
  expensive to run inside every pause. It is a separate program (`aw/pc_harm_readout.py`) that loads the saved
  `learner_end.ckpt` of a cell and scores the text with that exact cap. Every harm number in the deck comes from a
  reloaded checkpoint file.
- **The bounded-correction (mixture) study.** The mixture is applied at query time to a cap that already holds 300
  edits; that cap is the reloaded checkpoint of the realization-0 streams.
- **The worked examples** in this folder (`examples-*.md`, `examples.json`) show what each cap actually generated at
  the end-of-stream checkpoint, read from the saved files, with their hashes listed.
- **Audit.** Each checkpoint record carries the file's SHA-256 and the in-memory state hash. The freeze handoff lists
  the hashes, so anyone with the assets repository can reload a checkpoint and confirm they have the state the report
  describes.
- **Resource stops.** If a cell had to stop early for budget reasons, the completed prefix is exact and the `end`
  rescoring still runs, so the saved state and the numbers describe the same moment.

## 8. Why the immediate test and the checkpoint test are both needed

The immediate test (right after teaching) and the checkpoint test (after everything since) answer different
questions, and the deck's three retention words line up with them:

| Deck term | When measured | Question answered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Edit success | immediately after teaching that fact | can the cap store this fact at all? |
| Own-prompt retention | at a checkpoint, on the original prompt | is it still there after later teaching? |
| Paraphrase retention | at a checkpoint, on held-out rewordings | is it still *findable* under a new wording? |

The gap between the first and second rows is forgetting or interference. The gap between the second and third is the
reader's generalisation. Stable v0's 100 % own-prompt retention next to 0 % paraphrase retention on CounterFact is
exactly this: nothing was forgotten, but nothing could be found under a new wording either.

## 9. The other two senses, briefly

**The reader's training checkpoint ("the selected checkpoint is called v5").** The learned reader is a 3.35M-parameter
network trained by backprop for 300 optimiser steps, with its weights saved every 50 steps. v5 is not a single save: it
is the uniform average of the weights saved at steps 150, 200, 250 and 300 of one training run (seed 2), recorded in
`manifests/revision_v1/primary_condition_v5.json` with the file hash. Averaging late checkpoints is a standard way to
get a steadier network than any single step gives. Once selected, v5 was frozen; nothing about it changes during an
editing stream, and it is the same object in every cell that says "learned reader". The stream checkpoints of
section 4 then save the *memory* that v5 reads from, together with the (unchanged) v5 weights, so a reloaded cell is
complete.

**The base weight checkpoint ("Legacy 50M ePC base").** The ePC-trained GPT-2 used in the settling-control table is a
set of saved weights, produced by re-running the sibling repository's 50-million-token training recipe. Its file hash
is bound into the freeze, and every run that used it refused to start unless the file on disk matched the hash. The
ordinary BP base (the GPT-2 small on every other slide) is likewise identified by a digest of its parameters, checked
before and after every cell to prove it was never modified.

## 10. What a checkpoint is not

- It is not a point where anything is learned, selected or tuned. The cap is paused, examined and saved; teaching
  resumes from the identical state.
- It is not a retraining of GPT-2. The base has no checkpoints of its own during a stream because it never changes.
- It is not chosen by looking at results. The list was registered in the frozen manifest before the confirmatory runs.
- It is not the same as "the end" in general, though for 300-edit streams the two coincide and for 1,000-edit streams
  checkpoint 1,000 is the end.

## Glossary of the record fields (one entry per checkpoint in `checkpoints.json`)

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `tag` | `ckpt100`, `ckpt300`, `ckpt1000` or `end` |
| `items` | edits completed when the pause happened |
| `ret_es`, `ret_gs`, `ret_gs_n` | own-prompt and paraphrase retention over all facts so far; number of paraphrase scores |
| `survival_conditional_on_immediate`, `acquired_immediate` | retention among facts whose immediate test passed; how many that was |
| `locality` | complete-answer and first-token agreement with the base, mean KL, number of prompts |
| `drift` | mean loss difference and perplexity ratio on the fixed ordinary-text sample |
| `memory` | bytes allocated and occupied by the record store |
| `checkpoint_sha256`, `state_hash` | hash of the saved file; hash of the in-memory state, asserted unchanged after evaluation |
| `rows` | one line per taught fact with its own-prompt and paraphrase outcome at this pause |
| `rescoring_accel_seconds` | accelerator time charged to this pause, kept separate from learning time |
